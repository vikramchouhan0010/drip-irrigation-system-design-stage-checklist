# Drip Irrigation Design: Stage-by-Stage Checklist

Design a drip system in the order the water travels. A choice early in the chain fixes the next one: a 2 inch filter, for example, sets the size of the pipe it connects to.

## 1. Source and plot

Record the source (borewell, canal, pond, tank), pump flow and the pipe size it feeds, plot size, row and plant spacing, and whether fertigation through the line is wanted.

## 2. Filtration: choose for the water, not the farm size

- Hydrocyclone filter: removes particles above 75 microns; goes ahead of the main filter on sandy borewell water.
- Screen filter: sizes from 3/4 inch to 4 inch.
- Disc filter: finer filtering on water with mixed sediment.
- Sand filter: traps organic matter and algae from pond or canal water.

## 3. Fertigation and protection

A venturi injector draws liquid fertiliser using the pressure difference across it, with no external power. Fit a non-return valve wherever a pump or injector is used, an air release valve at high points, and a pressure relief valve (setting range 1.5 to 5.5 kg/cm2) against surges.

## 4. Control, mainline, laterals

Zone valves (manual or solenoid), compression and tee fittings, then drip tube or tape. Inline tube and tape carry emitters at 30, 40 or 60 cm spacing for closely planted rows.

## 5. Emitters

Where each plant needs its own emitter: 2, 4 or 8 LPH online drippers, or an adjustable dripper (0 to 70 LPH). A pressure-compensating dripper suits varying elevation.

## 6. Take-offs and line ends

Start connectors, end caps and flush valves. Lateral lengths, pressures and flow tables are not in a catalogue; confirm them in the quotation.
